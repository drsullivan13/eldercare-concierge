# North Jersey collection and release

Start with Union and Morris counties. Extend the same collection to Essex, Bergen, Hudson and Passaic. Keep dated snapshots for each source so that a change in its coverage can be distinguished from a new Harbor match. Record leads for Somerset and Middlesex separately.

## Repeatable workflow

1. Freeze a region definition and record the registry, release date, retrieval date, query or download URL, inclusion rule, and source row count for each care setting. Reconcile every in-scope source row to a Harbor ID or give its exclusion or unresolved reason. Check county against the physical address. For home services, record office geography and documented service geography separately.
2. Anchor nursing units and certified home health agencies to their CMS certification numbers. Anchor other licensed entities to state identifiers. Crosswalk aliases and campuses explicitly, keeping separately licensed units distinct. Preserve conflicting addresses with their sources and resolution. Test boundary ZIPs and unmatched coordinates before exposing radius search.
3. Join official evidence by verified identifier. Preserve metric dates, denominators, suppression codes, clinical measures, survey measures, regulatory warnings and penalties. Check provider-published offerings, room types, prices with billing periods, opening claims and contact numbers. Record an unknown when a claim cannot be supported. A call to check availability belongs in the later outreach phase.
4. Check every roster location for consumer listings. Log source, exact address and service match, checked date, directly observed rating, scale and review count, and source-family independence. Keep excluded candidates and coverage reasons. Apply the existing experience policy; report cataloged, checked, and rated counts by county and setting. A failed retrieval is a recorded access gap.
5. Freeze Facebook assignments before seeing the evidence. Use the [research prompt](north-jersey-facebook-prompt.md). Resolve provisional identifiers and stage observations for human review. Keep raw narratives out of scoring.
6. Run both markets through the same search, provider detail, regional coverage, shortlist and import entry points. Compare Massachusetts counts and samples with its previous release. Exercise NJ ZIPs, radius boundaries, category filtering, deduplication and score exclusions. Reconcile the released count tables to source manifests before publishing.

## Massachusetts lessons to carry forward

The Boston pilot had a single market baked into ZIP validation, a registry tied to the entire app catalog, and campus/unit ambiguities that required manual resolution. Its consumer listings sometimes represented syndicated data. For NJ, record region identity separately from provider identity, validate imports against their assignment and record scope, and resolve listing identity before scoring. Retain scored and unscored locations in the same coverage ledger. Capture actual source failures, corrections and successful refresh commands in the regional source manifests as collection progresses.
