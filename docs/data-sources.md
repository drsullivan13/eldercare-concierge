# Discovery data and search scope

This proof of concept contains **521 real public-source records** checked November 11, 2026: 280 CMS nursing facilities, 224 Massachusetts assisted-living directory entries, 10 home-care/home-health/private-staff agencies, and seven continuing/tiered-care campuses. Home-health quality and survey evidence was separately retrieved November 12. These are discovery candidates, not recommendations, independent vetting, a comprehensive directory, or confirmation that a particular person can receive care.

## Rebuild and retained inputs

Run `python scripts/prepare-data.py`. This deterministic, offline script reads retained normalized research inputs in `src/data/inputs/` and writes `providers.json` plus `catalog-meta.json`. It does not geocode an address or invent missing data. To refresh, retrieve and review the underlying sources, replace the relevant retained input, rerun the script, then review the data diff and tests. The retained input records preserve source URLs, dates, source flags, and additional fields beyond the display schema.

`providers.json` uses the shared `Provider` type. A missing numeric value stays `null`; a published zero stays `0`. CMS ratings and nursing-home metrics appear only on nursing facilities. Assisted living and home-care agencies do not receive borrowed CMS nursing-home stars or a proprietary composite score. `officialRecord` means an official listing was found; it is not a quality or current-availability endorsement.

## Nursing facilities

The [CMS Nursing Home Provider Information](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/dataset/4pq5-n9py) snapshot was released October 28, 2026, with processing date October 1, 2026, and retrieved November 11, 2026. The statewide research query contained 341 facilities. The regional source file retains 280 whose published point lies within 55 straight-line miles of Boston (42.3601, −71.0589).

Each display record links to the dataset and its specific Medicare Care Compare page. CMS overall/inspection/staffing/quality stars, staffing hours, turnover, latest standard health-inspection cycle count, published fine/payment-denial totals, abuse icon, special-focus status and source dates are retained. Ownership-change, old-inspection, abuse, special-focus and suppression footnotes become visible warnings where applicable. Penalty totals and latest-cycle deficiencies have specific CMS reporting windows; they are not lifetime histories or all complaint events. Source-level detail remains in the retained input, and current Medicare information should be checked directly.

## Assisted living

Source records combine the [Massachusetts assisted-living directory](https://www.mass.gov/assisted-living-residences), its linked residence pages/map points, and the [certified residences list dated June 2026](https://www.mass.gov/doc/list-of-certified-assisted-living-residences-as-of-june-2026/download), with list-as-of June 25, 2026. Statewide research covered 273 directory entries and 272 list rows; 224 directory map points fall within the pilot's 55-mile source region. Directory presence and certification-list matching are separate observations. Unmatched records and source anomalies remain warnings. Listed maximum occupancy is capacity, not an available-bed count. “Special care units” repeats the state field without independently establishing suitability for a particular diagnosis. No nursing-home CMS ratings apply.

## Home care, skilled home health and private staffing

The ten retained agency records contain provider websites plus trade-association, CMS or business-directory identity evidence. Source URLs and the exact supported claim appear on each record. Primary agency categories are six `home-health`, two `home-care`, and two `staffing`. All Care, Croí Health, ABC Home Healthcare and Privatus also have an explicit researched `home-care` search category for their personal/private care services. This preserves the primary category, returns each agency only once, and does not manufacture an agency quality rating. Privatus remains excluded from radius results because its office point is unconfirmed. Skilled Medicare home health is not the same promise as continuous private-duty personal care or nursing.

Provider service-area statements and town examples are shown as incomplete, requiring confirmation for the individual address. CMS historical OASIS service ZIPs indicate past patients, **not current coverage, staffing or acceptance**. Office point distance is not a service radius. Office geocodes use published addresses and the Census geocoder; a rejected ambiguous/wrong-street result stays null. Privatus therefore remains retrievable by saved ID but is excluded from radius results and the map. VNA Care's 250 Mount Vernon directions address is distinct from its 150 Mount Vernon mailing address, retained in source notes. Home-health CCN evidence identifies an entity without assigning nursing-home quality stars. Home agencies without independent regulatory proof have `officialRecord: false`.

## Continuing and tiered-care campuses

Seven real campuses are retained as `life-plan`: North Hill, Lasell Village, NewBridge on the Charles, Brooksby Village, Linden Ponds, Fox Hill Village and Carleton-Willard Village. The [Massachusetts continuing-care community directory](https://www.mass.gov/lists/continuing-care-retirement-communities), state-hosted filings, and linked provider pages support their identity and described care levels. `src/data/inputs/lifeplan.json` preserves care-level evidence, related care-unit identities and source uncertainty. State-directory scope is explicitly limited: it does not establish every unit license, current fees, admission/transfer rights, financial solvency or a campus quality rating.

The generated campus entries never contain a `cms` block. Their separately licensed nursing/assisted-living units retain separate records; nursing-unit stars, historical CCN measures and promotional star claims must never be inherited by the campus. North Hill's “enhanced living” and Carleton-Willard's supportive residential care are not relabeled as certified assisted living. Fox Hill's described inpatient rehabilitation is off campus. Related-unit names, addresses, IDs and CCNs remain in the campus limitations to make these distinctions reviewable.

Five campus entries use the actual published/matched street-address point supplied in research. Three of those points are reused from a separately listed CMS nursing unit **only when the street address exactly matches**; that location reuse transfers no quality measures. NewBridge and Brooksby have no reliable exact campus point. Their original null coordinates remain in retained research input; output uses the genuine Census representative point of ZIP 02026 or 01960 with `locationKind: 'zip'`, a separate Census source, and explicit description/limitation: “Exact campus point not confirmed; distance and map use ZIP area estimate.” These map/distance values refer to a ZIP area, not an entrance, campus boundary, or different-address licensed care-unit point. They can affect nearby cutoff decisions and require address confirmation. This narrowly authorized campus approximation is not applied to Privatus.

## ZIP coordinates and license

The local `ma-zips.json` contains 685 Massachusetts postal records, preserving five-digit strings and leading zeros. City/state labels come from the [GeoNames US postal export](https://download.geonames.org/export/zip/US.zip); its retained snapshot is licensed [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). **Attribution must remain:** “ZIP locations: U.S. Census Bureau (2026 Gazetteer); city names and additional postal points: GeoNames, CC BY 4.0. Massachusetts subset with coordinates combined from these sources.” Link GeoNames and the license wherever data attribution is displayed.

539 coordinate pairs are copied from the [2026 Census ZCTA Gazetteer](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/time-series/geo/gazetteer-files.html), fields INTPTLAT/INTPTLONG; 146 postal codes without a matching ZCTA retain their published GeoNames point. Census geographic data are U.S. government works; GeoNames attribution still applies to city labels and additional postal points. These are representative geographic points, not guaranteed mathematical centroids, address coordinates, or current USPS ZIP validation. Per-ZIP provenance and source SHA-256 manifests are retained alongside the data; `zip-data-sources.txt` gives further licensing and original-source details.

## Search contract

`GET /api/search` accepts `zip` (default `02459`), `radius` (default 25; 1–50 miles), `category` (`all` or a shared care-category key), `sort` (`distance` or `cms-rating`), and optional `q` (name/city substring, up to 200 characters). It returns the shared `SearchResponse`. Invalid/unrecognized/outside-Massachusetts ZIPs and origins more than 55 straight-line miles from Boston return HTTP 400 with an explanatory message. State-prefix screening does not imply Boston-area service coverage. The origin's requested circle can extend beyond the catalog's fixed 55-mile Boston boundary. Results are the intersection of the requested radius and the retained Boston-region catalog; a 50-mile search from Newton does not expand the catalog to every provider 50 miles from Newton. Missing results outside that catalog boundary are not evidence that no care exists there. This is a bounded pilot catalog, not a complete circle-wide census.

Radius filtering always applies, including CMS sort. Records without safe coordinates never receive a fabricated distance; they are excluded and counted for the selected category/text query. Category counts reflect located records matching each primary or researched supplemental category within the requested radius/text search, before the category filter. A provider can contribute to more than one category count, so their sum can exceed the unique result total. `catalog-meta.json` category counts remain primary-category counts. `total` is the returned count, `catalogTotal` is the complete retained catalog, and `asOf` is the November 11 research date (individual sources can be older). Ties use distance, then name, then ID. CMS sort is accepted only with category `nursing-home`, descending by published overall stars; missing stars sort last. This does not change the actual radius.

`GET /api/providers?ids=id1,id2` returns `{providers: Provider[]}` for up to 100 supplied IDs in requested order, omitting unknown/retired IDs and duplicates. Missing `ids` is HTTP 400; empty `ids` returns an empty list. This endpoint supports saved lists without running a new geography search and can return records with missing coordinates.

Tests: `npx tsx --test tests/search.test.ts`. They verify real-source ZIP points, leading zeros, radius inclusion, out-of-region handling, nursing-only rating sort/nulls, agency office exclusions, supplemental private-care matches, campus-versus-unit identity, five exact/supported campus points versus two sourced ZIP estimates, source coverage, numeric preservation and API validation.

## Individual CMS findings and penalties

`src/data/inputs/cms-inspection-details.json.gz` retains the actual retrieved Boston-region CMS detail extract. `scripts/prepare-inspections.py` decompresses it, checks provider identity and field values, and rebuilds `inspection-history.json` byte-for-byte. The application returns the latest five records initially, up to 25 per collection, while retaining the full snapshot counts. This view is intentionally bounded; source links point to the full CMS datasets.

- Health Deficiencies: https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/dataset/r5ix-sfxw
- Penalties: https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/dataset/g6vv-u9sr
- Scope/severity definitions: CMS State Operations Manual, Chapter 7, section 7203.3.2, https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/som107c07pdf.pdf#page=42

The A–L explanation is a direct mapping of the CMS grid; it is shown alongside the original source code. It describes the classification at that inspection. A correction date is a reported field, not an independent assessment of present conditions. Inspection cycles and penalty periods differ; the UI does not assert one uniform three-year window.

## Home-health clinical quality and patient experience

`src/data/home-health-evidence.json` preserves the October 21, 2026 CMS release, retrieved November 12. It joins seven exact Medicare certification numbers (CCNs) to six existing catalog organizations. VNA Care has two separately displayed agencies: Visiting Nurse Association of Boston (227068) and VNA Care Network (227098). The other matches are Mass General Brigham Home Care (227207), Beth Israel Lahey Health at Home (227495), South Shore VNA (227082), All Care VNA–Greater Lynn (227263) and Croí Health (227241).

A CCN identifies a Medicare-certified reporting agency and may cover multiple branches. It is not a rating of the nearest office, every service sold by the brand, a separate private-care business, or an entire corporate network. No network average is calculated, and these measures are never used as nursing-home stars.

The [Home Health Care Agencies dataset](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/dataset/6jpm-sxkc) supplies the quality-of-patient-care star rating and seven component measures. Half stars are preserved. The components in this release cover January–December 2025; each visible rate keeps its own period and favorable direction. Published risk-adjusted outcome rates are not reconstructed from observed numerators and denominators. The timely-start measure is a care-process measure.

The separate [HHCAHPS dataset](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/dataset/ccn4-8vby) covers April 1, 2025–March 31, 2026. It supplies survey stars, five published percentages, completion counts and response rates. “Overall care 9 or 10” is a response to a 0–10 question; “definitely recommend” retains that exact distinction. The seven agencies have 282–1,279 completed surveys and 17–26% response rates. Completion totals are not denominators for every question, and the published percentages include CMS adjustments. Results reflect respondents and prior care, not current capacity or an individual patient's likely outcome.

Missing and suppressed values remain null with raw CMS values and footnotes retained. A published zero remains zero. All seven current survey rows have published stars and no survey footnotes; that does not establish an unbiased sample. The [CMS dictionary](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/sites/default/files/data_dictionaries/home_health/HHS_Data_Dictionary.pdf) distinguishes insufficient clinical episodes, no current data, corrections, short survey periods, low survey counts and suppressed quarters. These reasons must not be collapsed into a poor-quality score. Forty completions is the survey-star eligibility threshold; the separate fewer-than-70 footnote is a caution, not the same threshold.

`python scripts/prepare-home-health.py` rebuilds the display snapshot from retained source rows, schemas, metadata and reporting-date files under `src/data/inputs/home-health/`. The API returns only an exact allowlisted catalog organization. Sources and clinical/survey periods remain distinct from the general November 11 catalog date.
